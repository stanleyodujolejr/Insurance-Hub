## Why this plan looks the way it does

Passing is not about hours logged. Three things move the needle, and this plan is built from only those three:

1. **Retrieval, not re-reading.** Answering questions beats reviewing notes. You already have 296 (MD) and 366 (VA) explained questions.
2. **Spending your time where you're weak.** Most people re-drill what they're already good at because it feels better. The progress report exists to stop that.
3. **A deadline someone else can see.** Studying alone with no check-in is where most licensing attempts quietly die.

The group session is not a lecture. It exists so nobody drifts.

## Daily: 20 minutes, on your own

Open the app. **Practice** tab. Do these in order:

**1. Clear your "Due for review" first.** If the red number on that button is above zero, start there. These are questions you have already missed, brought back at exactly the point you're about to forget them. Clearing this queue every day is the single highest-value 5 minutes in this plan. It is how something moves from "I got it right once" to "I actually know it."

**2. Then Random mix, 10 questions.** Read every explanation, even when you get it right.

**3. Ask about anything unclear.** Tap the **chat bubble** (bottom-right) and keep asking until it makes sense. Do not move on from a term you can't explain out loud.

**Dead time? Run flashcards.** The **🃏 Flashcards** mode drills the 155 key terms. Term on the front, definition on the back, you grade yourself. It uses the same schedule, so terms you miss come back. This is the one to do standing in line or waiting on an appointment. It builds the vocabulary that makes the scenario questions readable.

Twice a week, also switch to **By chapter** and run your *worst* domain from the last report.

Twenty focused minutes beats two distracted hours. If you only have ten, do the review queue and stop.

> **Why the queue matters:** a question you miss comes back tomorrow. Get it right and it returns in 3 days, then 7, then 14, then 30. Miss it again and it resets to tomorrow. Nothing leaves your rotation until you've proven you actually retained it.

## Twice a week: 45 minutes, together

Meet on a call or in person. Same agenda every time, no freestyling:

| Time | What |
|---|---|
| 0 to 5 min | Each person reads out their score on their **worst two domains** |
| 5 to 30 min | Work the single weakest domain **as a group**. One person pulls up questions and shares screen. Everyone answers out loud *before* the answer is revealed, then whoever got it right explains why. |
| 30 to 40 min | Each person names one rule they kept missing and teaches it to the others. Teaching it is the test of whether you know it. |
| 40 to 45 min | Each person states their target for the next session |

**The rule that makes this work:** you study the weakest domain in the room, not your own favorite topic. If Richard is at 40% on MD regulation, that's what the group does, even if Teyah is fine there. The concepts overlap more than you'd think, and explaining it out loud is what locks it in for her.

## Once a week: full timed mock exam

**Final Exam** tab, **Real exam** length, sit it in one go with no notes and no phone.

- MD Series 20-30: 130 scored + 10 unscored practice items / 150 minutes. The official pass mark is **70% of the scored items** (91). Life only is Series 20-27 and Health only is Series 20-24: 80 scored + 10 unscored / 105 minutes, same 70% of scored items.
- VA Series 11-01: 140 scored + 10 unscored practice items / 150 minutes. The pass mark on this page is **70% of the scored items** (98). That 70% is this practice page’s mark, not a cut score printed for Virginia. Life & Annuities is Series 11-05 and Health is Series 11-06: 90 scored + 15 unscored / 120 minutes, with the same page pass mark.

Treat it like the real thing. The score matters less than the domain breakdown it gives you, because that's what sets next week's focus.

## When you're actually ready to book the exam

Book it when **all five** are true:

- ✅ Two timed mock exams at **75%+** (aim above 70%, not at it, because test-day nerves cost points)
- ✅ **No domain below 65%**
- ✅ You've answered **at least 60%** of your question bank
- ✅ On your report, **Struggling is in single digits** and Mastered is climbing week over week
- ✅ You can explain, out loud with no notes: nonforfeiture options, the incontestability period, elimination vs probationary period, and your state's replacement rules

That fourth one matters more than raw accuracy. Accuracy counts lucky guesses and questions you'd already seen minutes earlier. **Mastered** only counts questions you got right after a two-week gap, so it's real retention, and it's what the exam actually measures.

If you're passing mocks at 71%, wait a week. The gap between 71% and 78% is about four study sessions, and it's the difference between a pass and a $60 retake.

## The two-week shape

| | Focus | Group | Mock |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Week 1** | Broad coverage. Get every domain *touched* so the report is complete | Tue + Thu | Sunday |
| **Week 2** | Only the domains under 70% from Week 1's report | Tue + Thu | Sunday |

If both mocks in Week 2 clear 75%, book the exam. If not, run Week 2 again with the new weak list. Repeating Week 2 is not failure, it's the plan working.
